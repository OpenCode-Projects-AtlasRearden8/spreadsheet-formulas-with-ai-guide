Chapter 1 · Asking in Plain English

You already know how to do this. Not with spreadsheets — with people. “You know how I keep losing my keys? Help me find a way to always know where they are.” That’s describing a problem in plain English, and it’s exactly how you talk to AI about formulas.

The single biggest misconception beginners carry is that they need to learn “a language” — formulas, functions, that sideways mountain =C2+B2 — before they’re allowed to ask for help. In 2026, you don’t. The AI writes the formula. Your job is to describe the need, then read and check what comes back.

What a “formula request” for a human sounds like

Imagine June’s candle spreadsheet from the intro. You want to know how much money January brought in. What do you tell a helpful human? Probably:

“Add up the money from all 12 orders — quantity times unit price for each row, and then total all of those.”

Notice what’s in that sentence:

  • The goal (total money from January)
  • The math rules (quantity × unit price per row, then sum)
  • The scope (all 12 orders)

A good AI prompt is basically that sentence, plus one more thing: a map of your sheet.

Step 1: Give the AI a map of your sheet

The AI can’t see your spreadsheet (unless you paste a screenshot or upload a file). So it doesn’t know whether “Price” is in column F or Q, or whether the data starts in row 1 or row 500. Fix this by describing your sheet in one compact block, like this:

My sheet has headers in row 1 and data in rows 2–13. Columns: A = OrderID, B = Date, C = Customer, D = Product, E = Qty, F = UnitPrice, G = Category, H = Region.

Paste that at the top of your chat, keep it pinned while you work, and every formula the AI writes for the rest of the session will line up with your real columns. Later chapters call this the sheet map.

Step 2: State the want in plain English

Now finish your request:

My sheet has headers in row 1 and data in rows 2–13. Columns: A = OrderID, B = Date, C = Customer, D = Product, E = Qty, F = UnitPrice, G = Category, H = Region.

In column I, I want each order’s line total (Qty × UnitPrice). Below the data, I want the total of all line totals.

Sit with this for a second, because it’s the whole book in miniature. You did not write =E2*F2. You described a job, the way you’d ask a coworker sitting next to you.

What comes back should look like this (column I, next to your data):

I2:  =E2*F2

…filled down through I13, and then, whichever empty cell you asked for:

I15: =SUM(I2:I13)

Type those in (or let the AI’s click-by-click instructions walk you), and you’ll see 584.00 — every dollar and cent January earned, 12 orders added up.

The test suite that ships with this project checks that number by re-adding the data from scratch; see tests/test_examples.py if you’re curious how a book knows its examples are right.

The prompt template worth memorizing

You can copy this one. It has four parts, and they never change:

I have a spreadsheet with [what it is]. [Sheet map: headers in row ?, data rows ?–?, column letters = names.] I want [the goal, in your own words]. Please give me the formula to put in [where], and tell me if I need to fill it down or adjust any cell references. Explain each part briefly.

Four parts: context, map, want, deliverables. You’ll see variations all through this manual, but the bones are always those four.

Weak prompt, strong prompt

Compare:

Weak Strong Why
“Make a formula.” “Add Qty × UnitPrice in column I for rows 2–13.” The AI can’t read minds; columns and rows matter.
“Do a VLOOKUP” “Look up each product’s price from my catalog sheet and put it in column J.” Names the data to look up and where to put it.
“Fix this: =SUMIF(…)” “This =SUMIF was supposed to be 294.00 but I get 0. My sheet map is [paste]. What’s wrong?” Gives intent + expected result + map. Chapter 2 lives here.

The strong column isn’t “formal.” It’s just the details a helpful human would need. If you’d hate to ask a coworker “do the thing,” don’t do it to your AI either.

Quick note: Excel, Sheets, or LibreOffice?

Formulas are nearly identical in Excel, Google Sheets, and LibreOffice Calc, and our examples all work in all three. Where they do differ (mostly cross-sheet references and a few newer functions like XLOOKUP), the chapter will flag it in a small note. If your AI knows which program you’re using, mention it in the prompt — “I’m using Google Sheets” — because that lets it pick the right syntax for you.

Your first worked homework (optional, low stakes)

Open examples/orders.csv and try, without looking ahead:

  1. Ask your AI (with the sheet map) for a formula in column I for line totals. Check two rows by hand: row for OrderID 1010 should be 3 × 42.00 = 126.00, and OrderID 1002 should be 1 × 18.50.
  2. Ask for the grand total in an empty cell. It should be 584.00.
  3. If either number came back wrong, tell the AI exactly that: “I got ___ but I expected ___ using manual math. Here’s my sheet map: …”

If all three went smoothly, you’ve already done the core loop of this entire book. On to Chapter 2 — reading what the AI wrote, out loud.
