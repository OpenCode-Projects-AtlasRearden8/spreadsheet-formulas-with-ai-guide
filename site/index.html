Welcome

Spreadsheet Formulas, With a Friend: A Beginner’s Manual for Learning with AI

Welcome. This manual is for people who look at a spreadsheet the way most of us look at a tax form: with respect, dread, and a strong desire to be anywhere else.

The good news: you do not need to memorize formulas anymore. You need to know two things:

  1. How to describe what you want, in plain English, to a chat AI.
  2. How to read a formula well enough to check it and fix it.

That’s it. That’s the whole skill. This guide teaches both, gently, with a single small example dataset you can follow from the first page to the last.

What you need: a spreadsheet (Excel, Google Sheets, or LibreOffice Calc — all work) and a free AI chat window. No cloud subscriptions, no installs, no coding. Everything in this book happens in two tabs: your spreadsheet and your chat.

Who wrote it, and why

This guide comes from Asdale Dynamics, LLC — Dylan Asdale, a registered nurse turned AI author. Everything here is written “like a friend would”: plain English, step by step, never condescending. It’s the formula companion to the interface guide, Spreadsheet Comfort Guide — that one teaches you where the buttons are; this one teaches you to get the spreadsheet to do math for you.

Meet the sample data

Every example in this manual uses the same tiny story: your friend June Tanaka sells handmade candles as a side business, and she has one spreadsheet of 12 orders from January 2026. (All names and orders are fictional examples, safe to copy.)

How to read this book

The chapters build on each other, but each one stands on its own too:

  1. Asking in plain English — the core skill: telling your AI what you want so it produces a formula that works the first time (most of the time).
  2. Reading and fixing a formula — how to read =SUMIF(...) out loud, and what #N/A and its friends are trying to tell you.
  3. VLOOKUP, without the fear — the famously scary formula, made small.
  4. SUMIF and COUNTIF — “add it up, but only if…” and “count them, but only if…”
  5. Dates without dread — dates are numbers wearing costumes. Once you see that, everything gets easier.
  6. Pivot tables, without the panic — the moment the drag-and-drop menu stops being a maze.
  7. Cleaning messy columns — ” maya chen “, OMAR HADDAD, and omar haddad become normal people again.
  8. Asking follow-up questions — the gentle loop of “does this look right?” that turns a formula into real understanding.
  9. Prompt patterns cheat sheet — one page to keep open while you work.

One rule, stated up front

Always keep a way to check the answer by hand. With 12 rows you can literally count on your fingers. Trust is built on small checks, and your AI should be able to explain its work to you, not just hand you magic. We’ll do exactly that all book long.
